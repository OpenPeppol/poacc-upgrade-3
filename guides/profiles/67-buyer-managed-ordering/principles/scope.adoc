= Scope

This BIS describes a process where the buyer is in control of the order.
The buyer issues an electronic order and may subsequently change or cancel that order, without the seller sending any business response.
The seller is expected to process and execute the last valid version of the order received.

Acceptance by the seller is implicit and governed by the underlying contract or framework agreement between the parties.
Because there is no response transaction in the process, the terms under which an order may be changed or cancelled – in particular any deadline after which
a change or cancellation is no longer accepted – are assumed to be agreed between buyer and seller before this process is used.

The main activities supported by this profile are

Order from buyer::
The Order transaction supports the structured ordering of goods or services, using free text or use of identifiers.
The information source of the ordered products may be a (paper or electronic) catalogue.

Change of order from buyer::
The buyer may change the order based on new requirements or other reasons.
Each Order Change replaces the previous version of the order in full and is sequenced by the buyer.
A change is sent before the order is prepared for delivery, or before any deadline agreed between the parties, see <<prerequisites>>.

Cancellation of order from buyer::
The buyer may cancel the order in full.
A cancellation is sent before the order is prepared for delivery, or before any deadline agreed between the parties, see <<prerequisites>>.

The following are explicitly *out of scope* of this BIS:

* Order Response (T76) and Order Response Advanced (T116). No seller response transaction is part of this process.
Sellers that need to respond to orders should use {link-bis-28}. Sellers that also need to respond to changes and cancellations,
or to propose changes themselves, should use {link-bis-65}.
* Changes or cancellations initiated by the seller.
* Order agreement, despatch advice, invoicing and any other post-award process. These are covered by their own BIS and may be combined with this BIS.

The following aspects are in scope on the same terms as in {link-bis-3}:

Accounting:: The ordering process must support the allocation of budgets, so the value amounts of the ordered products may be stated.

Invoice Verification:: The buyer may provide some information that the seller is required to place on the invoice for aiding and automation of invoice approval.

TAX reporting:: TAX reporting is not a general requirement on orders. In this context the term TAX is used as a generalization of taxes such as VAT, GST or Sales Tax.
TAX information is informative and does not affect the terms of trade.

Transport and delivery:: Only limited support is in scope for transport related information, but the buyer is able to provide information about requested delivery
location, some basic terms, time and contact persons for a delivery of an order.

Inventory:: Supporting inventory management is not in scope, but structured orders based on catalogues can be used to automate picking at supplier warehouses.
